# W1.3: point-in-time snapshot audit

**Task.** W1.3 from PHASE_5_6_PLAN.md, audit phase only.
**Date.** 2026-07-28
**Scope.** Audit and recommendations. No fix implemented. `demand.py`,
`config/assumptions.yaml` and all model parameters untouched.

---

## Summary

The backtest is honest about being a single fixed-vintage model and says so in
its own docstring. This audit's job was to establish how much that costs and
what it would take to remove. Two findings change the shape of the work.

**The largest apparent blocker is not a blocker.** US metro income, population
and GDP have no history in the warehouse, and I expected that to require new
ingestion. It does not. The BEA county files already downloaded and cached are
literally named `CAINC1__ALL_AREAS_1969_2024.csv` (56 annual columns) and
`CAGDP2__ALL_AREAS_2001_2024.csv` (24 annual columns). `ingest/census_bea_eia.py`
reads them, computes `sorted(years)[-1]`, and discards the rest. Recovering 56
years of point-in-time covariates requires no download and no new source.

**The real blocker is carrier and geography identity over time**, which W1.2
surfaced and nobody has costed. Carrier codes are reused, capacity purchase
agreements move between mainlines, and CBSA boundaries are redrawn. These have
no effective-dating anywhere in the codebase.

There are **11 identified leakage vectors**. Three are severe, four moderate,
four low. None invalidates the current backtest's published conclusion, which is
that demand rank alone does not predict survival, because that conclusion is a
null result and leakage would if anything inflate apparent skill.

---

## Current snapshot methodology

There is no snapshot machinery. `backtest/run_backtest.py` scores every launch
with one model:

- **Gravity coefficients**: fit on 2018-2019 US DB1B (`pre2022` vintage). For
  launches from 2021 onward this is legitimately pre-decision.
- **Transfer factor**: `per_hub_median` from `transfer_factor.yaml`, anchored to
  the frozen 2018 StatCan survey, estimated once.
- **Covariates**: current vintage throughout.
- **Candidate set**: metros within 250 to 3,000 miles with population at or
  above 250,000, evaluated at current population.
- **Scoring**: modeled demand percentile within the hub's candidate set.
- **No per-launch decision date is used at all.** Launches spanning 2021-12-15
  to 2025-06-14, a 3.5 year window, are all scored with the same model.

The module docstring already discloses the covariate and transfer-factor
lookahead. This audit confirms that disclosure is accurate and finds it
incomplete in the ways listed below.

## Leakage vectors

Ranked by severity: how much the vector could inflate apparent model skill.

### Severe

**L1. Covariates are current-vintage for every historical decision.**
`metro_anchor_points()` reads `dim_metro_us`, which holds one vintage:
BEA CAINC1 2024 income and population, CAGDP2 2024 GDP, Census 2023 delineation.
Scoring a December 2021 launch uses 2024 population. Because gravity is
`pop_a x pop_b` driven, a metro that grew fast between the decision and now is
credited with demand it did not have. **Direction of error: inflates apparent
skill**, because fast-growing metros are also where launches succeed.
*Already disclosed in the docstring.*

**L2. The candidate-set population floor is applied at current vintage.**
`population >= 250_000` decides which metros form the percentile denominator.
**59 metros sit within plus or minus 20% of that floor.** A metro that crossed
250,000 after a launch date silently enters the historical candidate set,
shifting every percentile computed from it. This is more insidious than L1
because it changes the denominator, not just one numerator, so it moves scores
for launches in unrelated markets.

**L3. Carrier identity is not effective-dated.** W1.2 established that
`F8` in BTS is Freedom Airlines (defunct 2010), not Flair (`07Q`); that Porter
files under both `PD` and `P3` with `P3` appearing only from 2024; and that
Swoop (`WO`) merged into WestJet in October 2023. Neither
`data/reference/carrier_codes.csv` nor `carrier_families.csv` carries effective
dates. Worse, `models/carriers.segment_brands()` resolves multi-brand regionals
using **DB1B year 2024** and the trailing T-100 window, then applies that map to
every year. Air Wisconsin flew for American until 2023 and United after; Mesa's
American agreement ended in 2023. Any pre-2023 competitive reconstruction using
today's brand map attributes capacity to the wrong airline.
*Not currently exercised by the backtest, which scores demand only. It becomes
severe the moment W1.4 replays full verdicts.*

### Moderate

**L4. CBSA delineation is fixed at the 2023 vintage.** Only `list1_2023.xlsx` is
downloaded. Census redrew metro boundaries in 2013 and 2023 among others.
Counties move between CBSAs, and CBSAs split and merge, so a 2005 metro is not
the same geographic object as its 2023 namesake. Affects both the covariates and
the airport-to-metro rollup.

**L5. The transfer factor is estimated once, with current covariates.**
Anchored to the frozen 2018 survey, which is correct, but
`models/transfer.estimate()` computes `gravity_pred` from
`metro_anchor_points()`, so current-vintage population and income enter the
denominator of every transfer ratio. The factor is then applied to all vintages.

**L6. Decision date is not modelled; launch date is used.** For the 44 register
rows carrying both dates, the median announce-to-launch lead is **102 days**
(p25 77, p75 182, max 304). A network planner commits roughly 3 to 6 months
before the first flight, so even a correctly vintaged model scored at launch
date sees a quarter of data the decision could not. **23 rows have no announce
date at all.**

**L7. Economics inputs are pinned to current vintage.**
`comparator_rates(year=2024)`, `fare_curve(con, year=2024)`, and
`fuel_price_base()` over the trailing 365 days. Fuel in particular swung
violently across the backtest window, so a 2022 launch scored at today's fuel
price is scored against economics that did not exist.
*Not exercised by the current backtest. Blocks W1.4 verdict replay.*

**L8. Served-market and competition windows are hardcoded to latest.**
`models/candidates.py` and `models/competition.py` both compute
`max(year*100+month)` and look back 12 months. A historical replay needs the
window ending at the decision date, otherwise the incumbent set includes
carriers that had not yet entered.

### Low

**L9. Route-spell status is as-of data end.** `dim_route_spell.status` marks a
spell operating if it is active within `REPORTING_LAG_MONTHS` of the panel end.
Correct for today, meaningless as of a historical date without recomputation.

**L10. Anchor airport selection uses 2024 seats.** `metro_anchor_points()` pins
`WHERE year = 2024`. A metro whose busiest airport changed is represented by the
wrong coordinates and distance for historical decisions. Small effect.

**L11. Register selection bias, unchanged by any fix.** `launches.csv` is
hand-compiled from news coverage and skews to newsworthy carriers; Flair and
Lynx supply most ceased rows. W1.2's derived register (89,502 spells) is the
remedy, and it is orthogonal to vintage correctness.

## Known limitations that no snapshot work removes

- **Positive-unlabeled structure.** Routes considered and rejected are
  unobservable. Precision against airline decisions is not interpretable as
  model accuracy. Pre-committed in PHASE_5_6_PLAN.md W1.6.
- **The oracle is weak.** Airlines launch bad routes; this repo's own
  post-mortems document two.
- **Covariate revision.** BEA revises historical estimates, so using today's
  file for 2015 gives the revised 2015 value, not the value a planner saw. This
  is irreducible without archived vintages and is a mild lookahead even after
  L1 is fixed.
- **DB1B and T-100 schema drift** across 35 years, and the 2020 exclusion, which
  the W1.2 spell bridge handles but which leaves a genuine hole in any 2020
  decision replay.

## Recommended fixes, priority ranked

Priority is expected reduction in leakage per unit of effort. **None of these is
implemented here.** All are Phase 6 or later, and each needs a change report
against the frozen W1.4 baseline.

| # | fix | addresses | effort | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keep all year columns in `_county_bea`, build `dim_metro_us_pit` | L1, L2, L5 | **1 to 2 days** | Data already cached. Highest value per hour in the entire W1.3 scope. |
| 2 | Preserve StatCan CMA history (2001-2022 already ingested) | L1 for Canadian hubs | **0.5 day** | `dim_metro_ca` takes `max(REF_DATE)` and discards 21 years already in the warehouse. |
| 3 | Effective-date `carrier_codes.csv` and `carrier_families.csv`; vintage the DB1B year in `segment_brands` | L3 | 3 to 4 days | Blocks any historical competitive reconstruction. Do before W1.4 verdict replay, not before a demand-only backtest. |
| 4 | Parameterise the T-100 window and DB1B year by decision date | L7, L8 | 2 to 3 days | Mechanical but touches four modules. |
| 5 | Score at decision date, defaulting to launch date minus a documented lead | L6 | 1 day | Use `announce_date` where present (44 of 74 rows); document the assumption for the 23 rows without one. |
| 6 | Download historical CBSA delineation files and vintage the rollup | L4 | 3 to 5 days | Genuinely fiddly: county reassignment must be handled, not smoothed. |
| 7 | Re-estimate the transfer factor per vintage | L5 | 2 days | Only meaningful after fix 1. |
| 8 | Recompute route-spell status as-of date | L9 | 0.5 day | Cheap, low value until W1.4 uses it. |

**Trivial and isolated fixes were considered and none were applied.** Fix 2 is
close to trivial but changes `dim_metro_ca`, which feeds `map_ca_airport_metro`,
the transfer factor and the backtest. Under the Milestone A rule that the
baseline must not move while it is being frozen, it belongs in Phase 6 with a
before and after.

## Effect on the current published result

The backtest reports operating routes at median percentile 0.89 against ceased
at 0.86, and concludes demand rank alone does not predict survival.

Leakage vectors L1, L2 and L5 all bias toward **overstating** model skill.
The published result is a null, so the leakage does not manufacture the
conclusion; if anything it means the true separation is weaker still. That is
worth stating plainly in `docs/backtest.md` when W1.4 lands: **the honest reading
is that the null result is robust to the leakage, not that the leakage is
harmless.**
