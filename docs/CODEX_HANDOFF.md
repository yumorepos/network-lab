## D. The Phase 6A decision

Specification: `docs/phase6_demand_model_redesign.md` section 3.
Evidence: `docs/phase6a_poisson_comparison.md`.
Decision and reasoning: `docs/phase6a_implementation_report.md`.

### What was built

`models/gravity.py` became a dispatcher over two estimators sharing one
functional form and one design matrix:

- `models/gravity_log_ols.py` (`log_ols`) - the Version 1 baseline, extracted
  intact. Writes `gravity_{vintage}.yaml`.
- `models/gravity_poisson.py` (`poisson_qmle`) - Poisson pseudo-ML, log link,
  mandatory HC1 robust covariance. Writes `gravity_{vintage}_poisson.yaml`.

`DEFAULT_ESTIMATOR` is `log_ols`, overridable by the environment variable
`NETWORK_LAB_GRAVITY_ESTIMATOR`, which exists so the counterfactual can run
through the real pipeline rather than a reimplementation of it.
`tests/test_phase6a_poisson.py` asserts the default has not moved.

### What Poisson improved

log-OLS fits `ln(pax)` and predicts `exp(Xb)`, which recovers the conditional
**median**, not the mean. Measured on the US calibration frame,
`sum(predicted)/sum(observed) = 0.439`: the baseline accounts for 44% of the
passengers that actually flew. Poisson QMLE targets the conditional mean
directly and its score equations force `sum(fitted) = sum(observed)` on the
fitting sample by construction.

| metric | log_ols | poisson_qmle |
|---|---|---|
| aggregate pred/obs, `current` holdout | 0.4352 | **0.9481** |
| aggregate pred/obs, `pre2022` holdout | 0.4508 | **1.046** |
| RMSE, `current` | 233,700 | **168,100** (-28%) |
| calibration, top predicted decile | 3.4x off | **1.1x** |

### What Poisson worsened

| metric | log_ols | poisson_qmle |
|---|---|---|
| median APE, `current` | **0.6153** | 0.7402 |
| MAPE, `current` | **1.050** | 1.849 |
| RMSLE, `current` | **1.001** | 1.128 |
| transfer-factor sd of ln | **0.915** | 1.130 |
| transfer median (published number) | **0.80** | 0.31 |

This is a trade, not a defect. Level metrics reward mean-targeting; relative
metrics reward median-targeting. Each estimator wins the metrics aligned with
its own objective.

### It did not meet all of its own pre-declared success criteria

not have to rediscover it. `docs/phase6_demand_model_redesign.md` section 3

| pre-declared criterion | outcome |

| holdout median APE no worse than 0.62 | **not met** (0.740 `current`, 0.722 `pre2022`) |

The spec also predicted the opposite of what happened: it warned that "demand
estimates rise materially, so verdict counts will move", with "up to 4 markets
could become LAUNCH-eligible", and called it "the change most likely to alter
the headline result". Zero verdicts moved. The reason is structural and is the
most important finding in Phase 6A.

### Why it is not the default, and the structural interaction with transfer

**Independently reproduced during this handoff**, not taken on trust. The
counterfactual was re-run through the real pipeline with
`NETWORK_LAB_GRAVITY_ESTIMATOR=poisson_qmle` and the baseline artifacts were
restored afterwards and verified against `git`.

Reproduced result: **zero of 200 verdicts change.** WestJet 0/0/76, Alaska
0/0/45, Porter 1/1/77 with the same named Boston LAUNCH and Hartford MONITOR.

The mechanism has two parts, and both matter:

**1. Partial algebraic cancellation through the transfer factor.** The transfer
factor is estimated as

```
transfer_i = observed_2018_i / gravity_pred_i        (pre2022 vintage)
```

and transborder demand is

```
demand = gravity_pred(current) x transfer x growth
```

For a **uniform** rescaling of gravity by `k`, `transfer` scales by `1/k` and,
because the median is scale-equivariant, the per-hub median factor does too. The
product is then exactly invariant. This is a real identity and it is the reason
improving gravity's level calibration cannot, by itself, improve transborder
demand under the current architecture.

Measured: the transfer factor falls almost exactly in proportion.

| hub | log_ols | poisson_qmle | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| national median | 0.8019 | 0.3064 | 0.38x |
| YYC | 1.2772 | 0.5038 | 0.39x |
| YYZ | 0.7784 | 0.2650 | 0.34x |

**But Poisson is not a uniform rescaling, so cancellation is only partial.**
Measured directly during this handoff, the `poisson/ols` prediction ratio across
US market pairs has median 1.40 but IQR [1.13, 1.77], range [0.20, 5.63], and
sd of the log ratio 0.36. It changes the cross-sectional shape as well as the
level. Consequently the raw pre-cap product does *not* come back to 1.00x:

| study | median product ratio, poisson / ols | sd of ln ratio |
|---|---|---|
| westjet_yyc | 0.485 | 0.242 |
| porter_yyz | 0.687 | 0.293 |

**2. The gravity path is discarded for every transborder market anyway.** This
is what actually pins the verdicts, and it is the stronger of the two effects.
Under the baseline:

| study | anchored (gravity bypassed) | capped at the ceiling | `gravity_x_transfer` |
|---|---|---|---|
| westjet_yyc | 24 | 52 | **0** |
| porter_yyz | 58 | 21 | **0** |

All 73 capped markets receive one identical value per hub (4,659 at YYC, 3,729
at YYZ) with standard deviation exactly zero. Anchored markets use
`anchor x growth` and never call gravity. So gravity's transborder contribution
is currently zero by two independent routes. Alaska SEA is 45 of 45 observed
DB1B, so gravity never fires there either.

Under Poisson, `gravity_x_transfer` fires for the first time on **14** markets
(12 WestJet, 2 Porter) that fall below the ceiling. All move **down**, by up to
72.5%. Reproduced exactly during this handoff.

**Take the precise statement, not the slogan.** "Transborder demand is
structurally invariant to any *uniform* rescaling of gravity" is true and is the
right thing to design around. "Nothing downstream moves because the transfer
factor cancels it" is too strong: the product does move, and the reason verdicts
do not is that the anchor rule and the survey cap discard the product first.

### What evidence would justify adoption

The implementation report recommends adopting Poisson **together with Phase 6B**
(censored estimation below the 4,000 threshold), because 6B is what lets the
gravity path actually drive transborder demand instead of being bypassed by the
cap. Concretely, adoption is justified when any of these holds:

1. **Phase 6B lands.** Once capped markets receive distinct per-market
   estimates, gravity calibration starts doing real work and the level fix has
   somewhere to land. Adopt both together, with one change report covering both,
   so the transfer-dispersion regression is judged against a transfer factor
   that is no longer a pure ratio to an uncalibrated model.
2. **W3.2 lands** and the transfer factor becomes a modeled effect rather than a
   ratio-to-model median. That removes the cancellation identity at its root.
3. **Phase 6B is deferred indefinitely.** Then adopt Poisson standalone anyway.
   Shipping a demand model that recovers 44% of actual passengers is worse than
   shipping one with a 20% higher median APE, and the current default should not
   be defended on the strength of metrics that reward median-targeting.

Adoption in any of those cases requires a change report, a named list of every
market that moves category, and a new baseline freeze, because the published
transfer median moves from 0.80 to 0.31 and appears in the README and the
validation docs.

## F. Known traps

Every item here is verified, either in the integrity report, the audit, or
during this handoff.

### 1. Bidirectional demand versus one-way departures

`demand_pax_yr` is bidirectional (DB1B O&D and the StatCan anchor both count A
to B plus B to A). `freq_wk` is one-way departures from the hub. Annual
departures are `freq_wk * 52 * 2`. The shipped formula omitted the factor of 2
and scored every route in every study at roughly twice its true load. Pinned by
a golden test against SEA to ICT (64.1 observed passengers per departure).
**Any new code dividing demand by departures must respect this.**

### 2. Operating certificate versus marketing brand

T-100 reports who flew the metal. DB1B reports who sold the ticket. SkyWest
(884,171 departures), Republic (369,160) and Envoy (334,585) never appear once
as a DB1B ticketing carrier. Joining one-stop itineraries on the operating
carrier made every mainline-plus-regional itinerary invisible and understated
competition. Air Canada was split across three codes at YYZ. Resolve through
`models/carriers.py` and `data/reference/carrier_families.csv`. The invariant
that exposed it: modeled carrier shares summed to 0.790 per market against an
observed 1.000; after the fix, 0.996.

### 3. Circular incumbent-network validation

`data/reference/incumbent_network_202604.csv` was presented as an independent
control and was in fact a dump of the same query it was checking: all 99 rows
matched with a maximum departure delta of 0.0, so
`test_no_screened_market_is_already_served` could not fail. It is now rebuilt by
`warehouse/incumbent_reference.py` from sources independent of the candidate
filter, and every row records how independent it actually is via
`independent_of_candidate_filter` (`alaska_sea` fully independent from DB1B;
`westjet_yyc` and `porter_yyz` only `partial`). **A cross-check that cannot
disagree is not a cross-check.** Do not "simplify" this back into one query.

### 4. DB1B versus T-100 grain

T-100 is segment data, DB1B is O&D data. Never join as if the same grain. T-100
has no Canadian domestic segments at all, which is why Air Canada connecting
itineraries out of Canadian hubs are structurally invisible (LIMITATIONS item
14). No amount of better BTS querying closes that; it is a disclosure-regime
consequence.

### 5. Stale generated artifacts

- **CI never regenerates.** `.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs only `pytest tests/
  -q`. The consistency tests therefore validate documents against **committed**
  artifacts, never freshly computed ones. A code change that alters results
  passes CI as long as the committed Parquet is not refreshed. Always run
  `make all` locally before trusting a green CI badge.
- **`make models` does not rebuild the Poisson artifacts.** `models/run_all.py`
  calls `gravity.fit(v)` with the default estimator only, so
  `gravity_current_poisson.yaml` and `gravity_pre2022_poisson.yaml` are refreshed
  only by `python -m models.gravity` or by a run with the environment override.
  They are inside the reproducibility gate, so a drift will be caught, but they
  can silently age relative to the log-OLS pair.
- **The Phase 6A comparison document is not auto-generated.**
  `docs/phase6a_poisson_comparison.md` says "generated by
  `scripts/phase6a_compare.py`", but that script prints to stdout and writes no
  file. The tables were transcribed. Re-run the script and re-check the numbers
  before citing them as current.

### 6. Floating-point Parquet byte churn

**Byte equality of Parquet is not a reproducibility contract and must not be
used as one.** `make all` rewrites 14 tracked Parquet and CSV files with float
last-bit noise of order 1e-15 relative, from parallel float summation in DuckDB.
Verdicts, chosen frequencies, gauge, demand and share are bit-identical; only
late decimals of margin and contribution move. Confirmed again during this
handoff.

The real contract is the content comparison in `scripts/check_reproducible.py`,
which joins on keys, tolerates 1e-6, and covers Parquet, CSV and YAML. The
sha256 fingerprint table in `docs/W1.4_baseline_freeze.md` carries a correction
notice saying exactly this; every one of those hashes moved on a plain rerun
with zero content change.

**Practical consequence:** after `make all`, a dirty tree of those 14 files is
expected. Verify the content is unchanged, then `git checkout --
data/parquet/outputs/` rather than committing noise.

### 7. The transfer-factor structural issue

Covered in full in section D. Short form: the transfer factor is a ratio to the
gravity model, so any uniform rescaling of gravity cancels out of transborder
demand exactly, and any non-uniform change is partly absorbed. **Until the
transfer factor stops being a ratio to the same model it corrects (W3.2), the
transborder studies cannot benefit however good the gravity model becomes.**
Compounding it, the anchor rule and the survey cap currently discard the gravity
product for all 155 transborder candidates, so gravity's live transborder
contribution is zero.

### 8. The cap reports a bound as a point estimate

All 73 capped markets receive one identical value per hub with standard
deviation exactly zero, and the screen then emits that many separate PASS
verdicts with per-market drivers as though each had been analysed
independently. Statistically valid as an upper bound; wrong as an estimate.
Decision-relevant: removing it would put 1 WestJet and 3 Porter markets over the
hurdle. This is what Phase 6b targets.

### 9. Hub income is a destination-income proxy

`models/demand.py` sets `inc_a = inc_b = destination metro income` because
`dim_metro_ca` has no income column. With a fitted `ln_income_product`
coefficient of 1.0257 this **doubles the destination-income elasticity**. The
docstring discloses the substitution and argues the level error cancels through
the transfer ratio, which is correct for the level; a scalar cannot correct a
slope. W3.3 fixes it.

### 10. Point-in-time leakage

11 vectors catalogued in `docs/W1.3_point_in_time_audit.md`, 3 severe. The
backtest is a single fixed pre-2022 model with current-vintage covariates and
discloses the lookahead. **Do not quietly refit per vintage without saying so.**
BEA and StatCan ingests keep only the latest year, so historical covariates are
not currently recoverable from the warehouse.

### 11. `dim_route_spell` is not a persisted table

`PHASE_5_6_PLAN.md` W1.5 lists `dim_route_spell` as a required table and
`docs/W1.3_point_in_time_audit.md` refers to `dim_route_spell.status`. **No such
table exists in the warehouse.** The register is derived on demand by
`warehouse.route_spells.build()`, which takes about ten seconds over the full
T-100 panel. It is also not yet wired into the backtest, which still scores the
74-row hand-compiled `data/reference/launches.csv`. Confirmed during this
handoff: 89,502 spells, 657 carriers.

### 12. The counterfactual override has no automated restore

Running with `NETWORK_LAB_GRAVITY_ESTIMATOR=poisson_qmle` overwrites tracked
artifacts **and** regenerates `docs/validation.md` with Poisson numbers. There is
no make target that restores the baseline afterwards. Back up
`data/parquet/outputs/` first, and finish with `git checkout --
data/parquet/outputs/ docs/validation.md`. Running `make reports` while in the
Poisson state would bake non-default numbers into reader-facing documents.

### 13. README still implies `BELF_MAX` lives in `assumptions.yaml`

`README.md`'s threshold table is headed "Threshold (assumptions.yaml)" and lists
"BELF cap 0.85". `BELF_MAX = 0.85` is hardcoded at `models/screen.py:19` and is
**not** in `config/assumptions.yaml`. This is a known open item, catalogued in
`AUDIT_2026-07-27_VERIFIED.md` section 6.1 and tracked there as P2 item 14. It
was **not** fixed in this handoff because the fix is either a config change (out
of scope here) or part of the deferred README rewrite. The audit lists 13 other
rule-1 violations alongside it.

### 14. Environment

`requirements.txt` and CI pin Python 3.11; the local `.venv` in this working
copy is Python 3.12.6. Everything passes on both, but a version-sensitive result
would show up locally and not in CI.

## H. Corrections made in this handoff

Two, both current-state only. No model, config, artifact or default was touched.

### 1. `PROGRESS.md` published a backtest N of 42 against a computed 48

The canonical numbers block read "backtest N = 42 launched-and-resolved of 61
scored". `headline_metrics.yaml`, `docs/backtest.md`, `README.md`,
`docs/interview_story.md` and `INTEGRITY_REPORT.md` all say 48, and the artifact
computes 48 (27 operating plus 21 ceased, of 61 scored). 42 is the value
produced by reading the `status` column instead of `outcome`, a bug that
`models/run_all.py` documents in a comment as having been caught once already.
Corrected to 48.

### 2. The consistency guard covering `PROGRESS.md` was checking nothing

`tests/test_consistency.py::_live_text` split `PROGRESS.md` at the first
`## Phase ` heading, on the assumption that the canonical block was the only
content above the historical log. When the Phase 6A entry was added at the top
of the file it became the first `## Phase ` heading, so the function truncated
`PROGRESS.md` to its 15-character title line and every guard below it checked an
empty document. That is how the wrong N survived.

The marker is now `## Phase 0`, the fixed first entry of the chronological log,
so current-state sections added at the top stay inside the checked region. The
backtest-N pattern was also made whitespace-tolerant, because `PROGRESS.md` wraps
between the count and the phrase and a literal-space pattern matched nothing
there.

**This widens what the guard checks; it does not relax any assertion.** Verified
in both directions, per the repository's own discipline that a test never shown
to fail is a test of nothing: with the defect reintroduced the repaired guard
fails with "PROGRESS.md states 42 launched-and-resolved, computed 48", and with
the correction in place the full suite is 45 passed.
