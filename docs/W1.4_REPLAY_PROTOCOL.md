# W1.4 replay protocol

**Status.** Frozen 2026-08-14, **before the primary replay was executed**. The
metric code had not been written when this document was committed; see the
disclosure in section 8 for the partial outputs that had been observed during
implementation.

**This is a prospective analysis plan, not a blind preregistration.** A great
deal about this model's behaviour was already known, and section 8 lists it.

**Amended reporting contract.** Per the 2026-08-14 amendment in
`PHASE_5_6_PLAN.md` W1.4, all four families are attempted and reported; FULL
cells get inferential analysis with intervals; DESCRIPTIVE_ONLY cells get
estimates and counts without forcing an underpowered model; NOT_ESTIMABLE cells
get the reason, the counts and the blocking limitation. **Feasibility
classifications may not be upgraded by widening a window or pooling studies.**

## 1. Eligible universe and decision dates

### 1.1 A Stage 1 correction that tightens Stage 0

Stage 0 set the snapshot floor at 2019-01 from **cache coverage**: DB1B starts
2018Q1 and Form 41 starts 2018. It did not apply **publication lag**. A decision
at 2019-01 can only read a calendar year whose Q4 was published before it, which
is reference year 2017 for both sources, and neither is cached.

The corrected floor is **2020-01**, and with 2020 and 2021 excluded as decision
vintages, **2022-01**.

| study | Stage 0 decision dates | corrected | reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| westjet_yyc | 2019, 2022, 2023, 2024 | **2022, 2023, 2024** | 2019-01 needs 2017 DB1B and Form 41; not cached |
| alaska_sea | 2022, 2023, 2024 | **2022, 2023, 2024** | unchanged |
| porter_yyz | 2024 | **2024** | unchanged; gauge from 2023-11 |

Seven study-by-date snapshots, not eight.

### 1.2 Frozen cutoffs

Decision dates are 1 January. Each snapshot resolves every source through
`replay/vintage.py`, which fails closed rather than reaching forward.

Emitted by `replay.snapshot.resolve_vintages`, not transcribed by hand:

| cutoff | T-100 through | DB1B | Form 41 | BEA | StatCan CMA | anchor | gravity | seats |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022-01 | 2021-10 | 2019 | 2019 | 2020 | 2020 | 2018 | pre2022 | 2019 |
| 2023-01 | 2022-10 | 2021 | 2021 | 2021 | 2021 | 2018 | pre2022 | 2021 |
| 2024-01 | 2023-10 | 2022 | 2022 | 2022 | 2022 | 2018 | pre2022 | 2022 |

The seats year is the latest **complete** T-100 calendar year inside the
permitted window, so at a 2022-01 cutoff it is 2019: 2020 is excluded from the
warehouse entirely and 2021 runs only through October within the window. DB1B
and Form 41 skip 2020 for the same reason and land on 2019.

`pre2022` (2018-2019) is the only gravity calibration published at any approved
cutoff. The production default `current` (2023-2024) is future data at all
three and is refused by the resolver.

### 1.3 Eligible universe

The candidate set **generated at the cutoff**, not today's. Every rule is the
study's own: distance band, 400,000 metro population floor, 250,000 annual
airport seat floor, satellite rule, and the metro-level exclusion of markets the
study carrier already served in the trailing twelve reported months.

**The universe is positive-unlabeled.** A market that did not see a launch is
**unlabeled, not a confirmed negative.** Carriers decline good markets for fleet
and capital reasons that this model does not represent. No precision figure may
be reported without that caveat in the same table.

## 2. Outcomes and horizons

**Outcome source.** The derived route-spell register
(`warehouse/route_spells.py`), restricted to the study's brand family, its hub,
and US destinations, rolled up to metro.

**Family 1 outcome.** A candidate metro is a **positive** at cutoff `t` if the
study carrier began a spell to any airport in that metro with `first_ym` in
`(t, t + 24]`. Horizon **24 months**, frozen.

**Family 2 outcomes.** Horizons **24 and 36 months**. A launch is an *event* if
its spell ended before `first_ym + horizon` **and** ended more than two months
before the panel end (2026-04); otherwise it is **censored**. The two-month
margin is `REPORTING_LAG_MONTHS` from the register.

**Corporate-shutdown treatment.** Recorded as a distinct cessation cause and
excluded from route-level event counts. Stage 0 established there are **zero**
such events in all three studies: all three carriers still operate, and Swoop,
the one corporate event in range, flew no in-scope YYC to US segments. The
field is retained so the count is visible as zero rather than absent.

**Seasonal treatment, and this is the one that matters.** A spell is seasonal
when it has internal gaps. Stage 0 found 21 of 35 eligible launches seasonal.
**A seasonal stop is not a business failure**, so every survival result is
reported split by `seasonal_flag`, and the pooled figure is never reported
alone.

## 3. Duplicate and repeated-observation treatment

A metro appears at up to three decision dates, and those rows are **not
independent**. Two consequences, both frozen here:

1. The primary Family 1 and Family 4 statistics pool all decision rows, and a
   per-date breakdown is reported alongside.
2. **All uncertainty is computed by a cluster bootstrap resampling METROS, not
   rows**, 2,000 resamples, percentile interval. Resampling rows would treat the
   same market at three dates as three independent observations and would
   understate the interval.

Where a metro launches more than once in a window (a re-entry), it counts
**once** as a positive.

## 4. Ranking score, and why a verdict cannot be the ranker

**The verdict carries almost no ordering information**: 198 of 200 current
candidates are PASS. Ranking on a three-level label with one populated level
cannot produce a curve.

Frozen decision:

- **Primary ranking score: base-case `margin_pct`** at the selected frequency
  and gauge (fare scenario 0, fuel scenario 0). Continuous, and it is the
  quantity the verdict thresholds.
- **Secondary: `annual_contribution`**, reported alongside.
- **The verdict itself is reported as a confusion table**, not as a ranker.

## 5. The four families

### Family 1: observed launch agreement, 24 months

- `observed_launch_agreement_at_k`: of the top `k` candidates by ranking score
  at cutoff `t`, the fraction that launched within 24 months.
- `observed_launch_recall`: of the metros that launched within 24 months, the
  fraction inside the top `k`.
- `k` frozen at **5, 10 and 20**, all reported; no `k` is selected afterwards.
- Baseline for comparison in Family 4: the same metrics with markets ranked by
  `demand_pax_yr` percentile.
- Reported per study and per decision date, then pooled with a metro-cluster
  bootstrap interval.

### Family 2: survival

- Kaplan-Meier by verdict where verdicts vary, otherwise by ranking-score
  tercile, split by `seasonal_flag`.
- **No Cox model and no Fine-Gray model is fitted.** Stage 0 measured twelve
  events pooled across three studies, eleven of them Alaska. This is frozen in
  advance so that a favourable-looking split cannot later motivate a regression.
- Report eligible, events, censored, seasonal, and the reason wherever the cell
  is NOT_ESTIMABLE.

### Family 3: right-sizing

- Compare the model's `chosen_freq_wk` at the last decision date **preceding**
  the launch against the observed opening frequency.
- **Frozen primary proxy: `first3_max`**, the maximum one-way weekly frequency
  over the first three active months. This matches `initial_freq_wk` in the
  register, so the replay measures the quantity the register already publishes.
  `first_month` and `first3_median` are reported as secondaries.
  The three disagree on 72% to 79% of spells and move the target by 28% to 46%,
  which is exactly why the choice is frozen here rather than made later.
- Metrics: signed bias (`chosen - observed`), ratio (`chosen / observed`), MAE,
  and the share of launches where the model chose the minimum of the option list.
- Split by study and by `seasonal_flag`.

### Family 4: incremental value over demand rank

- The **same rows, dates, candidates, cutoffs and outcomes** as Family 1. No
  separate sample is constructed.
- Full model (ranked by `margin_pct`) against demand percentile (ranked by
  `demand_pax_yr`), on agreement at k and recall at k.
- The reported quantity is the **difference** in each metric, with a
  metro-cluster bootstrap interval on the difference.
- **If the full model does not beat demand rank, that is the finding and it is
  published.**

## 8. known_before_protocol

Everything below was known when this protocol was frozen.

1. **Current verdicts:** WestJet 0/0/76, Alaska 0/0/45, Porter 1 LAUNCH
   (Boston) / 1 MONITOR (Hartford) / 77 PASS. 200 candidates, 1 LAUNCH.
2. **Right-sizing:** the model selects 3x weekly on **200 of 200** candidates.
   Observed opening medians are 6.0 (WestJet), 14.3 (Alaska), 14.1 (Porter).
   The direction and rough magnitude of the bias is known in advance.
3. **Porter Boston:** model 3x, actual 14x on 2026-05-14.
4. **The existing demand-rank backtest is null:** 61 scored, 48 resolved,
   operating 0.89 against ceased 0.86, +0.03 in every cut.
5. **Route-spell register:** 89,502 spells; 90.7% reproduction of the hand
   register, Wilson interval roughly [80%, 96%].
6. **Phase 6A:** Poisson QMLE moved zero of 200 verdicts.
7. **Transborder structure:** `gravity_x_transfer` fires zero times; WestJet 24
   anchored and 52 capped, Porter 58 anchored and 21 capped; all 73 capped
   markets take one value per hub with standard deviation exactly zero.
8. **Alaska is 45 of 45 observed DB1B**, so gravity never fires there.
9. **Eleven leakage vectors**, three severe.
10. **StatCan anchor is a 1997-2018 panel** and its publication floor moves by
    vintage (280 to 3,090), not a constant 4,000.
11. **Stage 0 feasibility counts**, including Alaska's single Family 1 positive
    and Porter's five at one date.

### 8.1 Partial primary outputs already observed. Disclosed.

Honesty requires naming these rather than claiming a clean freeze. While
building and debugging `replay/snapshot.py` the following were run and seen:

- **Snapshot at production vintages**, all three studies: reproduced the
  committed 76 PASS / 45 PASS / 1 LAUNCH + 1 MONITOR + 77 PASS. This is the
  parity check and it is a reproduction of published numbers, not new evidence.
- **Snapshot at the 2024-01 cutoff**, all three studies. Candidate counts 76
  WestJet, 43 Alaska, 88 Porter, and verdict counts all-PASS for WestJet and
  Alaska and **2 LAUNCH / 86 PASS for Porter**.
- **Candidate counts at 2022-01**: WestJet 76, Porter 77.

**No metric family was computed.** No agreement at k, no recall, no survival
curve, no right-sizing bias and no full-model-against-demand-rank comparison
existed in any form when this document was frozen. The observed items above are
inputs to those metrics, not the metrics.

The one item that could bias a later choice is Porter's 2 LAUNCH at 2024-01.
It is disclosed here, and the ranking score, the `k` values, the bootstrap
design and the feasibility classifications were all frozen in sections 3 to 6
above before any metric was computed.

## 9. Leakage guard demonstration

Every guard was shown to fail when its leak is reintroduced.
Reproduce with `.venv/bin/python scripts/demo_leakage_tests.py`.

| guard | injection | fired |
|---|---|---|
| T-100 window never reaches past the cutoff | `t100_max_ym` forced to the panel end 202604 | yes |
| DB1B year complete and published by the cutoff | `db1b_year` forced to the production 2024 | yes |
| Form 41 year complete and published | `form41_year` forced to 2025 | yes |
| Gravity calibration never from the future | resolver replaced with one that picks the newest window without checking publication | yes |
| Gravity calibration never from the future | `gravity_vintage` forced to the production `current` | yes |
| Geography lookahead declared exactly when it applies | `GEOGRAPHY_RELEASE_YM` back-dated to 1900 | yes |
| Vintage policy vocabulary enforced | `Provenance` built with an unknown policy string | yes |
| Porter has no served metros before its network existed | served-market window upper bound moved to the panel end | yes |
| Candidate sets actually move with the cutoff | every cutoff resolved to production vintages | yes |
| PIT transform fails closed on a missing cache | `CAINC1` pointed at a nonexistent file | yes |

**10 of 10.** Two defects in my own implementation were caught by these guards
before the protocol was frozen: the geography guard originally keyed off the
calendar year, so a 2023-01 cutoff read as clean while using a delineation not
released until 2023-07; and the first gravity injection proved nothing, because
it tested that the resolver refuses an unpublished window, which it correctly
does.

## 10. Amendment rule

**The primary protocol is not modified after results are seen**, except to fix
a disclosed implementation defect. Any such fix is recorded in this document
with its date, what was wrong, and what changed. Adding a labelled sensitivity
is permitted and never replaces a primary result.
